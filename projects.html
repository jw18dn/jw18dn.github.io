Public Projects

Below are a culmination of projects I was the lead developer on that can be spoken about publicly.

Tendon-Driven Robotic Hand

This project took a tendon-driven hand from a single motor on a bench to all five fingers and a wrist under coordinated control on hardware. Two overlapping tendon controllers were consolidated into one optimization-based controller and one direct map, and the wrist's closed kinematic chain was folded into the same solver so fingers and wrist are commanded through a single interface. A persistent oscillation was traced to resonance near the command rate, where the tendon and its termination behave as a spring, and was resolved by changing the motor operating mode. Remaining tracking error was tied to solver weighting rather than the formulation itself, and command-path lag was cut four-fold by widening the setpoint filter and handing the position loop to the drives. A separate analysis proved that the diagonal tendon routing loses control authority on the ring and pinky, reframing what had been read as a tuning problem as a structural limit of the routing.

Sensorless Hand State Estimation

The hand carries no joint sensors, so its only knowledge of its own pose comes from an estimator that maps measured motor positions to joint angles. An iterative solve that diverged under load was replaced with a direct inverse map, and a tendon-stretch correction driven by measured tension was added and characterized on the real hand rather than assumed from the model. Two errors that had quietly corrupted every prior result were root-caused: the estimator double-counted wrist motion, and a slack tendon caused logs to report good tracking on a finger that was visibly missing its commanded pose. Supporting this is a calibration suite — a five-pose routine an operator walks through that serves as the single source of truth for the motor-to-tendon map, two autonomous routines that find each joint's travel limits with no operator present, and per-hand motor zeros with tendon tension recorded at every capture pose so a calibration can be reproduced and scored against a range-of-motion report.

Real-Time Hardware Integration

This work built the real-time foundation the hand runs on. Three motor bus types were brought up behind a single interface, with two driven simultaneously on the same hand, and the hand was integrated onto a humanoid platform over one EtherCAT connection with a containerized build and run path. The servo control rate was raised from 20 Hz to 250 Hz by tracking down a USB latency default and a per-motor reply delay, and real-time scheduling with CPU pinning was landed alongside a test proving the control tick performs no dynamic allocation. Drive provisioning now writes the full parameter set to every motor and stores it persistently, so limits survive a power cycle or a drive swap — the fix for a class of failures that included calibration torque pinned low by a stale drive-side limit and an over-current threshold left with no headroom for normal transients. Tendon force was standardized to newtons everywhere above the drive layer, replacing three competing units and the silent conversions between them, and an always-on recorder captures drive faults and dropped packets per motor so a failure can be read back rather than scraped from console history.

Hand Simulation and Tactile Sensing

Both hardware revisions of the hand were brought into simulation with correct tendon routing and a shared scene, backed by a test that proves the two models stay in step as the hardware evolves. A complete simulated tactile fingertip was built, modeling the path from contact on the compliant pad through the readout electronics, so sensing behavior can be developed and evaluated before the physical sensor is available. To keep the physics engine off the real-time path, a static control map is generated offline and evaluated directly in the control loop.

Teleoperation and Operator Tooling

This project made the hand drivable and observable by anyone on the team. Motion-capture-glove and webcam teleoperation were built on one shared retargeting layer, so either input can drive the simulation or the physical hand without a separate pipeline. A browser control panel launches every simulation and hardware run with live logs and an embedded 3D view, replacing memorized command lines. Reporting turns each run into a readable result — power draw, current dwell curves, and per-joint tracking tables — and the shared-memory pipeline feeding the robot state visualizer was mapped and scripted for both simulation and hardware.

Grasp Force Control and Humanoid Calibration

Alongside the tendon-driven hand, this work addressed grasp force and joint calibration on other platforms. A commercially available anthropomorphic hand was characterized on a purpose-built load-cell testbed, producing measured maximum finger output forces and a direct two-hand comparison that became the evidence base for the fix: a force supervisor that stopped grasp force from oscillating and gave each finger its own limit. On the humanoid, a joint-zeroing routine was built with gravity compensation, probing in both directions, an operator gate, and mirrored joint pairs probed together. A lever-arm transform was also derived to correct measurements from a relocated inertial sensor back into the original sensor frame.

Blind Legged Locomotion Improvements

This project was multifaceted and focused on improving the existing code structure as well as enhancing blind locomotion behaviors. The goal was to rely solely on proprioceptive sensing, without vision, to improve system stability. The experimental results shown in these videos demonstrate that the proposed approach and methodology improved performance when the system encountered both large and small unexpected curbs. Bipedal Force Control

This project develops a force-based double-support balancing controller that enables a bipedal robot to remain stable on uneven, compliant, and dynamically changing terrain using only minimal model information. The controller commands pelvis motion and ground reaction forces, which are converted into joint torques via a model-free PD controller, allowing the robot to balance robustly despite disturbances such as moving platforms, soft surfaces, and sudden drops. Experimental results demonstrate reliable balance performance on the Tallahassee Cassie platform, highlighting the potential for robust locomotion with reduced dependence on detailed robot dynamics models.

Convex Obstacle Avoidance

This project presents a real-time motion planner that enables robots to avoid multiple moving obstacles without requiring knowledge of their dynamics or intentions. The planner uses convex model-predictive control with a half-space relaxation to maintain convexity while approximating obstacle distances, allowing fast and reliable trajectory generation. Iterative refinement and soft penalty formulations improve avoidance performance, enabling agile maneuvers in dynamic environments. The method is validated in simulation with legged locomotion and in hardware with quadcopter drones, demonstrating successful real-time avoidance of fast and unpredictable obstacles.

Through Contact Motion Planning

This project presents a convex model-predictive motion planner for legged robots that generates real-time behaviors without predefined contact sequences or gait timing. The planner iteratively updates both the robot trajectory and predicted ground contacts, enabling dynamic through-contact motion while maintaining fast convex optimization. Simulations show that the robot can naturally achieve stable hopping, transition between behaviors, and reject disturbances, with gait characteristics emerging from the robot dynamics rather than manual tuning. These results demonstrate efficient real-time locomotion planning with minimal reliance on predefined contact patterns.

Legged Locomotion in Resistive Media

This project develops a real-time gait optimization method that enables legged robots to run efficiently through fluid-like terrains such as sand and other resistive media. By using a reduced-order dynamic model and smooth optimal control formulation, the approach computes energy-efficient running gaits online with fast nonlinear optimization. Simulations and hardware experiments on a monopod robot demonstrate effective locomotion through granular substrates, validating the method's ability to generate efficient gaits in challenging, deformable environments.
